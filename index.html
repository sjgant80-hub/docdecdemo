The grounding logic is a plain, auditable function over your documents' distinctive terms — you can read exactly why it answered or refused, and the citation points at the paragraph that decided it. (The production engine deepens this routing privately on your own hardware; the behaviours you see — grounds, cites, abstains — are the same.)

Why it matters — for each role

For the person asking

An answer you can trust because it shows its source — and a straight "that's not in your documents" instead of a confident invention. The failure mode of RAG (a fluent wrong answer) is the one that costs you.

For Compliance / Risk

Every answer is auditable: it cites the exact paragraph, or it abstains. Grounded-or-silent is the property a regulator, a lawyer, or an auditor actually asks for — and the one a nearest-chunk retriever can't give.

For IT / Security

Sovereign. The documents and the index stay on your hardware; nothing is sent to a vendor cloud per query. The grounding runs on CPU — no per-question API bill.

For the data owner

Point it at your corpus; it answers from that, cites it, and refuses what isn't there. Replace a disappointing RAG at the retrieval seam without re-platforming.

2 · Sit it beside your RAG

Honest limits — the part a sales page usually hides

Grounding is a trade, and this benchmark shows it. On SQuAD 2.0's answerable questions DocDec's recall is a little below the vector-RAG's — a grounded router that refuses weak matches answers fewer questions than one that always guesses. In exchange it answers with higher precision, cites every answer, and abstains on questions the documents don't cover, where the RAG hallucinates on every single one.

And it is not a magic no-hallucination box. SQuAD 2.0's "unanswerable" questions are adversarial — written to look answerable from a specific paragraph — so a transparent term-grounding router is fooled on many of them (the exact rate is in the table above, not spun). On genuinely off-topic questions — the common real case — the match score collapses and it abstains cleanly. The honest summary: DocDec moves you from "always answers, often wrong" to "answers with a citation, or admits it doesn't know," and the measured abstention strengthens as the routing deepens in the production engine. Those numbers are a public-corpus demo, not a promise about your documents — we'd measure on yours.
