DocDec v2 · the grounding layer

Answers only from your documents. Shows the exact words. Refuses the rest.

DocDec sits between your people and your documents. Ask it a question and it finds the passage that answers it, copies the answer word for word from that passage, and shows you where it is. If no passage supports an answer, it says so instead of guessing. It runs on your own machine. The first version of DocDec was caught out. A member of the AI Craftspeople Guild re-ran our benchmark and showed that, at the same setting, it did no better than a plain cosine similarity search with a tuned cut-off, and refused fewer unanswerable questions. Its refusals came from that cut-off, not from its structure. We re-ran the comparison on the same questions: …

Words used on this page
RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)
The usual way to put AI over documents: cut them into chunks, turn each chunk into one list of numbers (a vector), find the chunks whose vectors sit closest to the question's, and ask a language model to answer from them.
Single vector / cosine
One vector per passage; "closeness" is the cosine of the angle between two vectors. Fast, but a whole paragraph squeezed into one point loses the specific word a question hinges on.
Late interaction (ColBERT)
One vector per word-piece instead of per passage. Every word of the question looks for its best match anywhere in the passage, and the passage's score adds those best matches up. A missing specific term shows up as one weak match instead of being averaged away.
The gate
The checks an answer must pass before it leaves. Each refusal names the check that stopped it.
The reader
A small language model running on your machine. It is only allowed to copy the answering phrase out of the passage, or say NONE. The gate then checks the phrase really is in the passage, word for word. The model is never the source of an answer.
Tune split / eval split
Every setting is chosen on a first slice of the questions, in file order (tune). Every result is reported on the rest (eval), which the settings never saw.
McNemar test
A standard test for two systems answering the same questions: it looks only at the questions where they disagree and asks whether one wins those more often than chance would give. A claim passes only below p = 0.05.
Unanswerable / near-miss
SQuAD 2.0's unanswerable questions were written by people to look answerable from a specific paragraph that does not actually contain the answer. They are the hardest refusals there are.

The frontier prototype: hyperbolic memory (measured, not claimed)

The idea: put the corpus inside a curved space (a Poincaré ball) where room grows exponentially toward the edge, as a tree branches. General things sit near the centre, specific things near the edge, so "how specific is this" becomes a distance. The published version trains a model to do it. We built the training-free version (direction from the ordinary embedding, radius from depth: article, paragraph, sentence) and measured it against plain cosine on a mixed index of articles, paragraphs and sentences. It is never a product claim either way.

npm install                         # transformers.js (tokenizer), onnxruntime-node, a parquet reader

Blueprint: how it is built, part by part

Each part is a small, pure module with no network access. Each is mutation-tested: a tool rewrites the code one operator at a time (< to <=, && to ||, and so on) and the tests must catch every change. A change the tests miss is a line nobody really checked.

For your business

In one breath

DocDec gives your people answers from your own documents that they can check in seconds: the exact words, the passage they came from, and a refusal when the documents do not support an answer. It runs on hardware you already own, so the documents never leave the building and there is no per-question bill.

What it does for each role

Chief executive

An AI answer you can put in front of a customer or a regulator, because it shows its source and refuses rather than invents. The failure that ends careers, a fluent wrong answer believed, is the one it is built to prevent.

Finance

No per-question API bill and no per-seat AI subscription for this job. The comparison below is computed from sourced, dated list prices and your own volume. The running cost is electricity on machines you already have.

Operations

Answers come with the exact sentence, so checking one takes seconds instead of re-reading a page. When a policy changes, the new version supersedes the old one on its date, and nobody is told the old rule.

Technology and security

Runs offline on CPU: a 34 MB token-matching model and a 7-billion-parameter local reader. No document, question or answer is sent to any vendor. Pure modules, mutation-tested, MIT-licensed: you can read, run and keep all of it.

Compliance, risk and legal

Every answer is an audit record: question, source, version, the exact character offsets of the words relied on, and the date. Every refusal names the check that stopped it. Old versions are closed, never deleted, so "what did we say was true on that date" has an answer.

The teams who use it

Ask in plain words. If the answer is there you see it highlighted in its passage; if not, you are told so straight, and the refused question is logged as a gap in the documents someone can fill.

The cost, this against that

A cloud setup sends every question and its passage to a hosted model and bills per token. DocDec does the same job on your own machine. Set your volume; every price is read from the price lock (copied from the estate's sourced registry, each with its source and the date it was checked), and every quantity is measured on this page.

What it replaces

A cloud RAG stack: a hosted embedding service, a vector database subscription and a hosted language model billed per token, with the documents copied to each. DocDec replaces the whole answer path at the retrieval seam. Your document stores stay where they are; DocDec indexes them where they are.

Rolling it out

  1. Your corpus, your questions. Index a slice of your own documents on a machine you own. Write down real questions, including ones you know the documents do not answer.
  2. Measure it against what you have. Run the same questions through your current setup and through DocDec with this page's harness: wrong answers, refusals, citations. Your numbers, your call.

White paper: subsuming RAG instead of replacing it

The problem. The usual RAG pipeline cuts documents into chunks, embeds each as one vector, retrieves the nearest chunks to the question and asks a model to answer from them. Its failures are structural, not a matter of tuning. Cutting into chunks breaks facts apart. One vector per passage blurs the specific term a question hinges on. Retrieving once misses questions that need two facts. A frozen index cannot tell a superseded fact from a current one. And nothing stops the model answering confidently from the wrong text.

The approach. Keep cosine retrieval, but only as the cheapest first step, and surround it with layers that each fix one failure:

  • L1, late interaction (ColBERT, Khattab and Zaharia, SIGIR 2020), here the 33-million-parameter answerai-colbert-small-v1, int8, on CPU.
  • L2, a graph over the corpus's own entities, so a two-fact question becomes a walk.
  • L3, a dated memory: versions with validity windows, verified answers with their provenance, re-checked before reuse, and pruned when unused.
  • L4, a verifier gate whose refusal is the product: a local reader may only copy, and the copy is checked word for word.
  • L5, a conductor that reads the question's shape and routes it.

The evaluation. The hypotheses, the sets, the selection rules and the pass rules were published before the held-out numbers were computed (PREREG.md). The sets:

  • SQuAD 2.0 dev (Rajpurkar, Jia and Liang, ACL 2018), whose unanswerable questions were written to look answerable: the guild's own corpus, then nine times the corpus.
  • HotpotQA (Yang et al., EMNLP 2018) for two-fact questions.
  • Changed facts in real paragraphs, for freshness.
  • Paraphrases for the memory.

The baseline is the one that caught the first version: cosine plus a cut-off tuned on the same data. Its prefix variant is chosen in its own favour. Every threshold is searched exhaustively on the tune split, and every claim is decided by an exact McNemar test on the eval split.

The results are on the claims board above. They are generated from the record, not typed here, so this paragraph cannot disagree with them: …

The frontier. Hyperbolic memory, with radius as specificity (Poincaré embeddings, Nickel and Kiela, NeurIPS 2017; the hyperbolic-RAG line of work, 2025), was prototyped without training and measured. Its result is above, whichever way it fell.

Reproducibility. The shipped data, the measurement code and the CI gate re-derive every number on every push. The encoders and the reader can be re-run from scratch on any machine with the published commands.

Honest limits

  • These are public benchmarks, not a client's documents. They show the mechanism and the method; your corpus gets its own measurement.
  • The freshness test changes real paragraphs synthetically: the new value is not meant to be true, only different. It tests the mechanism, not world knowledge.
  • The paraphrases were written by the local model, not by people.
  • The reader is a 7-billion-parameter model on a laptop CPU. A larger local model may read better; its numbers would have to be measured, not assumed.
  • The hyperbolic prototype is training-free. A trained version is the published idea; we have not built or measured one.
  • The speed figures were measured on one laptop. Yours will differ.
